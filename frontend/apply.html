🗣️ 8 Preguntas — English (click para respuesta)
1. "Walk me through your AP experience"
"For 7 years at Brinks International, I managed the complete AP cycle for Brazil and Caribbean operations in a shared services model. This included invoice intake and validation, three-way matching, coding, supplier reconciliation, payment execution, and monthly close. I handled multi-currency environments with different tax regulations, managed relationships with 15+ vendors, and ensured on-time payments aligned with cash flow priorities. I also built AP KPI dashboards using SQL and Power BI to track aging, throughput, and exceptions."
💡 Hit every keyword from the job description: shared services, invoice validation, supplier reconciliation, aging, KPIs.
2. "How have you led or supervised a team?"
"While my formal title was Analyst, I functionally led AP operations for two entire regions. I coordinated with local finance teams in Brazil and the Caribbean, ensured balanced workload distribution across payment cycles, and was the primary point of contact for vendor escalations. I trained colleagues on our reconciliation processes and took ownership of the entire workflow — from invoice receipt to payment execution to monthly close. This independent regional leadership is what prepared me for a formal AP Lead role."
💡 Address the gap head-on. Reframe regional management as functional leadership.
3. "Tell me about a process improvement you implemented"
"I noticed our payment tracking was entirely manual — spreadsheets updated by hand, no automated alerts. I built SQL queries that automatically cross-referenced invoice records against payment confirmations, flagging discrepancies in real-time. I then created a Power BI dashboard that visualized AP aging, throughput, and exception rates. This automation reduced payment tracking time by 15% and eliminated the manual errors that were causing delayed payments. It's the kind of digitalization initiative I'd bring to Monks."
💡 This directly answers their "support automation and digitalization initiatives" requirement.
4. "How do you handle vendor disputes?"
"At Brinks, I managed 15+ vendor relationships across two regions. My approach: first, verify the data — pull the invoice, PO, and receiving records for three-way matching. Most disputes resolve at this stage because they're data errors, not actual disagreements. For genuine disputes, I'd communicate directly with the vendor, present the documented evidence, and negotiate a resolution. I always maintained a professional relationship because these are long-term partnerships. I resolved 60% of historical outstanding disputes using this systematic approach."
💡 Shows systematic approach + relationship management + results (60%).
5. "How do you ensure compliance with accounting standards and tax regulations?"
"At Brinks, I managed AP for Brazil and Caribbean — each with different tax regimes. My approach was threefold: first, maintain updated knowledge of local tax requirements through collaboration with local finance teams. Second, build validation checkpoints into the process — every invoice was checked against tax compliance rules before payment. Third, maintain audit-ready documentation — organized records of invoices, POs, approvals, and tax certificates. We passed every audit during my tenure."
💡 Multi-country tax compliance experience is exactly what they need for shared services.
6. "What AP KPIs do you track and how?"
"I track aging buckets (current, 30, 60, 90+ days), payment throughput (invoices processed per week), discount capture rate, exception rate, and processing cost per invoice. At Brinks, I built these KPI dashboards in Power BI connected to our financial database via SQL queries. This gave leadership real-time visibility into AP performance. I'd bring this same data-driven approach to Monks — most AP teams rely on Excel; I can build automated reporting that updates in real-time."
💡 Naming specific KPIs shows you KNOW AP metrics. The Power BI angle is your competitive advantage.
7. "Why Monks? Why this role?"
"Three reasons. First, Monks operates at the intersection of technology and business — as a Systems Engineering student who automates AP processes, I'm aligned with that digital-first culture. Second, the shared services model is where I thrive — I managed Brinks' AP shared services for two regions. Third, the AP Lead role lets me formalize the leadership I've already been doing and scale it. I want to build an AP function that's not just efficient but data-driven and automated — and Monks' commitment to digitalization tells me you want the same thing."
💡 Connect your vision to their needs: digital-first, shared services, automation.
8. "What questions do you have for us?"
Ask these: • "How has the AP team and shared services center grown over the past year?" • "What ERP system does the AP team currently use, and are there plans for migration?" • "What does success look like for the AP Lead in the first 90 days?" • "How does the AP function interact with Treasury for cash flow planning?" • "What automation or digitalization initiatives are currently in progress or planned?"
💡 These questions show strategic thinking and genuine interest. The growth question also surfaces potential red flags.
